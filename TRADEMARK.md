# VigilOps Trademark Policy

"VigilOps" and the VigilOps logo are trademarks of the VigilOps project. The
code is open; the name is not.

These are two different things, and the difference matters:

- **The [MIT licence](LICENSE) covers the code.** It lets you use, modify,
  distribute and sell the agent, including commercially. We mean that, and this
  policy takes nothing back from it.
- **This policy covers the name.** A licence to use code is not a licence to use
  the name it came under. That's how people tell your software from ours.

## What you may do without asking

- Say your product "uses VigilOps", "is built on VigilOps", "is compatible with
  VigilOps", or "is a fork of VigilOps" — accurate statements of fact are always
  fine.
- Use the name in articles, talks, tutorials, comparisons and reviews, including
  critical ones.
- Keep the existing copyright and licence notices in copies of the code. The MIT
  licence requires this.
- Run a modified version internally, under any name, including ours.

## What you may not do

- **Name a fork or a derived product "VigilOps", or anything confusingly close**
  — "VigilOps Pro", "VigilOps Plus", "VigilOpsHQ", "Vigil-Ops", and so on.
  Pick your own name; "Built on VigilOps" belongs in the description, not the title.
- **Imply that we endorse, sponsor, maintain or support your version.** We don't,
  unless we've said so in writing.
- Use the name or logo in your company name, product name, domain name, app
  store listing, social media handle or logo.
- Use it in a way that suggests your distribution is the official one, or that
  users of your version are VigilOps Cloud customers.
- Modify the logo, or use it as your own.

The point is not to stop you forking. Fork away — the MIT licence says you may,
and we're not going to be precious about it. The point is that someone typing
"VigilOps" into a search bar, or running `curl … | sudo sh` from a page that
says VigilOps, should get software we actually published. This tool takes root
and the Docker socket on production servers; name confusion here isn't a
branding inconvenience, it's a security problem for the person installing it.
